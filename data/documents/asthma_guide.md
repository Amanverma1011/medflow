# Asthma Guide

*Synthetic demo document for MedFlow General Hospital. Educational content only.*

## What is asthma

Asthma is a common long-term condition that affects the airways in the lungs. The airways become inflamed and narrow from time to time, which makes it harder to breathe. Asthma can start at any age and varies from mild to severe.

## Symptoms

Typical asthma symptoms are wheezing, coughing, chest tightness and shortness of breath. Symptoms often come and go, and may be worse at night, early in the morning, or during exercise.

## Triggers

Common triggers include colds and viral infections, pollen, dust mites, animal fur, tobacco smoke, cold air, exercise and strong smells. Knowing your triggers helps you reduce flare-ups.

## Inhalers

Most people with asthma use inhalers. A reliever inhaler is used when symptoms occur to open the airways quickly. A preventer inhaler is used regularly to reduce inflammation and prevent symptoms. Your nurse or pharmacist can check your inhaler technique, which makes a big difference to how well the medication works.

## Asthma action plan

A written asthma action plan explains which medication to take every day, what to do when symptoms get worse, and when to seek help. Ask your care team for a plan and keep it somewhere easy to find.

## When to seek medical care

Seek emergency care immediately if a reliever inhaler is not helping, if you are too breathless to speak in full sentences, or if your lips turn blue. Book a review with your doctor if you need your reliever inhaler more often than usual.
