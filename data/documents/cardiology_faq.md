# Hospital Cardiology Guide

## Understanding blood pressure readings

Hypertension is generally defined as persistently elevated blood pressure measured on several occasions. A single high reading does not mean you have hypertension, because blood pressure rises naturally with activity, stress and pain. Your cardiologist looks at the pattern of readings over time.

## Common heart tests

An electrocardiogram (ECG) records the electrical activity of the heart and takes a few minutes. An echocardiogram uses ultrasound to show how the heart muscle and valves are working. An exercise stress test records the heart while you walk on a treadmill. A Holter monitor is a small recorder worn for a day or more to capture the heart rhythm during normal activity.

None of these tests is painful. Your cardiology team will explain which test is needed and why.

## Palpitations

Palpitations are a feeling that the heart is pounding, fluttering or beating irregularly. They are common and often harmless, and can be triggered by caffeine, stress, lack of sleep or exercise. Tell your doctor if palpitations are frequent, last a long time, or come with dizziness.

## Chest pain

Chest pain has many causes, and not all of them involve the heart. Because some causes are serious, new or unexplained chest pain should always be assessed by a clinician.

## Heart attack warning signs

Warning signs of a heart attack can include pressure, tightness or pain in the chest that may spread to the arm, jaw, neck or back, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea and light-headedness. Symptoms can be milder or different in women, older adults and people with diabetes.

## Preparing for a cardiology appointment

Bring your medication list, any home blood pressure record, and previous test results. Write down your questions in advance. Wear comfortable clothing in case an ECG or examination is needed.

## When to seek medical care

Call your local emergency service immediately for chest pain that lasts more than a few minutes, chest pain with breathlessness or sweating, fainting, or sudden severe shortness of breath. Do not drive yourself to hospital.
