# Diabetes Education Guide

*Synthetic demo document for MedFlow General Hospital. Educational content only.*

## What is diabetes

Diabetes is a long-term condition in which the level of glucose (sugar) in the blood is too high. It happens when the body does not make enough insulin, or cannot use insulin properly. Insulin is the hormone that moves glucose from the blood into the body's cells for energy.

Type 1 diabetes occurs when the body stops producing insulin. Type 2 diabetes, the most common form, develops when the body becomes resistant to insulin or does not make enough. Gestational diabetes can appear during pregnancy.

## What HbA1c means

HbA1c is a blood test that shows your average blood glucose level over roughly the past two to three months. It measures how much glucose has attached to haemoglobin, the protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen. Because red blood cells live for about three months, the result reflects a long-term average rather than a single day.

The HbA1c test does not usually require fasting. Your doctor uses it, together with other tests, to diagnose diabetes and to check how well treatment is working. Your personal HbA1c target is set by your care team.

## Common symptoms

Symptoms of high blood glucose can include increased thirst, passing urine more often, tiredness, blurred vision, slow-healing cuts and unexplained weight loss. Type 2 diabetes can develop slowly and some people have no symptoms at first. Symptoms alone cannot confirm diabetes; a clinician needs blood test results to make a diagnosis.

## Monitoring blood glucose

Your care team will tell you whether and how often to check your blood glucose at home. Wash and dry your hands before testing, and record your readings with the date and time. Bring your records to each appointment.

## Healthy living with diabetes

Balanced meals, regular physical activity, not smoking and attending regular reviews all help manage diabetes. Check your feet daily for cuts, blisters or colour changes, and attend eye screening when invited.

## Low and high blood glucose

Low blood glucose can cause shaking, sweating, hunger, confusion and palpitations. Follow the plan your care team gave you for treating a low reading. Very high blood glucose with vomiting, drowsiness, deep rapid breathing or abdominal pain needs urgent medical attention.

## When to seek medical care

Contact your diabetes team if your readings are often outside your target range, or if you are unwell and unable to eat or drink. Seek emergency care for severe drowsiness, confusion, or loss of consciousness.
