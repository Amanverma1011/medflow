# Hospital Hypertension Guide

*Synthetic demo document for MedFlow General Hospital. Educational content only.*

## What is hypertension

Hypertension is a condition where blood pressure remains consistently above the healthy range. It is also called high blood pressure. Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing against the walls of the arteries as the heart pumps.

A reading has two numbers. The top number, systolic pressure, is the pressure when the heart beats. The bottom number, diastolic pressure, is the pressure when the heart rests between beats. Hypertension is generally diagnosed by a clinician when readings stay raised across several separate visits, not from a single measurement.

## Causes and risk factors

High blood pressure can be influenced by several factors that often act together. Common risk factors include older age, a family history of hypertension, excess body weight, a diet high in salt, low physical activity, regular heavy alcohol use, smoking, and long-term stress. Some people develop high blood pressure because of another condition, such as kidney disease or a hormonal disorder.

Many people with hypertension have no identifiable single cause.

## Symptoms

Hypertension usually causes no symptoms, which is why it is sometimes called a silent condition. Most people only find out through a routine blood pressure check. Headaches, nosebleeds or dizziness are not reliable signs of high blood pressure.

## Monitoring blood pressure

The hospital recommends measuring blood pressure at the same times each day, for example morning and evening. Sit quietly for five minutes before measuring, with your back supported, feet flat on the floor and your arm resting at heart level. Avoid caffeine, exercise and smoking for 30 minutes beforehand.

Take two readings one minute apart and record both in a diary or app. Bring your record to every appointment so your care team can see the pattern over time. Use a validated upper-arm monitor with a cuff that fits your arm.

## Lifestyle measures

Healthy habits help keep blood pressure in range. These include reducing salt, eating more vegetables, fruit and whole grains, staying physically active most days, keeping to a healthy weight, limiting alcohol and not smoking. Your care team can help you choose changes that fit your life.

## Treatment

Treatment decisions are made by your doctor based on your readings, overall health and risk of heart disease. Some people manage blood pressure with lifestyle changes alone, while others also need medication. If medication is prescribed, take it exactly as directed and do not stop or change the dose without speaking to your prescriber.

## When to seek medical care

Contact your doctor if your home readings stay higher than the target your care team gave you. Seek emergency care immediately if very high readings come with chest pain, severe headache, shortness of breath, confusion, weakness on one side, or changes in vision.
