# Laboratory Test Preparation Guide

*Synthetic demo document for MedFlow General Hospital. Educational content only.*

## Preparing for a blood test

Most blood tests need no special preparation. Drink water as normal, because being well hydrated makes it easier to take the sample. Wear a top with sleeves that roll up easily. Bring your request form and photo ID.

Tell the phlebotomist if you feel faint with needles, take blood-thinning medication, or have had problems with blood tests before.

## Fasting blood tests

Some tests, such as a fasting glucose test and some cholesterol tests, require fasting. Fasting means no food or drink except plain water for 8 to 12 hours before the test. Your request form will state whether you need to fast.

Continue to take your regular medication with water unless your doctor has told you otherwise. Book fasting tests for the morning so most of the fasting time is overnight.

## Tests that do not need fasting

The HbA1c test, a full blood count, kidney function and thyroid tests do not usually require fasting. If you are unsure, ask the laboratory before your appointment.

## Urine samples

Use the sterile container provided. For a mid-stream sample, pass a small amount of urine first, then collect the sample without stopping the flow. Label the container and hand it in as soon as possible.

## After the test

Press firmly on the site for a few minutes to reduce bruising. A small bruise is common and fades within a few days. You can eat and drink as normal straight after a fasting test.

## Getting results

Results are sent to the doctor who requested the test, usually within one to three working days. Your doctor will explain what the results mean for you. Results are not interpreted by laboratory staff.

## Where to go

The laboratory collection centre is on the Ground Floor of the Main Building, next to the outpatient pharmacy. It is open from 7:00 AM to 5:00 PM on weekdays.

## When to seek medical care

Contact your doctor if the puncture site keeps bleeding, becomes very swollen, or looks infected.
