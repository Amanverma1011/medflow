# Medication Safety Guide

*Synthetic demo document for MedFlow General Hospital. Educational content only.*

## Taking medication safely

Take each medication exactly as prescribed, at the times and in the way described on the label. Keep an up-to-date list of every medication you take, including over-the-counter products, vitamins and herbal supplements, and show it to every clinician who treats you.

Store medication in its original packaging, away from heat and moisture and out of reach of children. Check the expiry date before use.

## Missed doses

If you miss a dose, check the patient information leaflet or ask a pharmacist what to do. Do not take a double dose to make up for a missed one unless your prescriber has specifically told you to. Taking two doses close together can increase the risk of side effects.

## Changing or stopping medication

Do not change the dose, skip doses or stop a prescribed medication without speaking to your prescriber or a pharmacist first. Some medication must be reduced gradually, and stopping suddenly can be harmful. If cost, side effects or a complicated schedule make it hard to take your medication, tell your care team so they can help.

## Side effects

All medication can cause side effects, although many people have none. Common side effects are listed in the patient information leaflet. Tell your doctor or pharmacist about any side effect that is troublesome or does not settle.

## Allergic reactions

Signs of a serious allergic reaction include swelling of the lips, face or throat, difficulty breathing, a widespread rash and feeling faint. This is a medical emergency: call your local emergency service immediately.

## Medication interactions

Some medication interacts with other medication, with certain foods, or with alcohol. Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about everything you take before starting something new, including products bought without a prescription.

## Antibiotics

Antibiotics treat bacterial infections and do not work against viruses such as colds and flu. Take antibiotics as prescribed, and do not share them or save them for later.

## When to seek medical care

Contact a pharmacist or your doctor if you are unsure how to take a medication. Seek urgent medical care if you think you have taken too much, or if a child has swallowed medication.
