# Patient Education Handbook

## Understanding common medical terms

Acute means a condition that starts suddenly and is usually short-lived. Chronic means a condition that lasts a long time. Benign means not cancerous. A diagnosis is the identification of a condition by a clinician. A prognosis is the expected course of a condition.

## Understanding vital signs

Vital signs are basic measurements of how the body is functioning. They include body temperature, pulse rate, breathing rate, blood pressure and oxygen saturation. Nurses check vital signs regularly because changes can be an early sign that someone is becoming unwell.

## Understanding blood pressure numbers

Blood pressure is recorded as two numbers, such as 120 over 80. The first number is the systolic pressure and the second is the diastolic pressure. Hypertension means blood pressure that stays persistently above the healthy range.

## Understanding common tests

A full blood count measures red cells, white cells and platelets. HbA1c shows average blood glucose over two to three months. A lipid panel measures cholesterol and triglycerides. Kidney function tests show how well the kidneys filter waste. An ECG records the heart's electrical activity.

## Preparing questions for your doctor

Write down your main concerns before the appointment and start with the most important. Useful questions include: What is my diagnosis? What are my options? What are the benefits and risks? What happens if I wait? Who do I contact if things change?

## Shared decision-making

You have the right to be involved in decisions about your care. Tell your care team what matters to you so they can help you choose the option that fits your life.

## Using this information

This handbook provides general education. It does not replace advice from a qualified healthcare professional who knows your medical history.
