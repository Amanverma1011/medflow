# Fever in Children Guide

*Synthetic demo document for MedFlow General Hospital. Educational content only.*

## What is a fever

A fever is a body temperature that is higher than normal. In children it is usually a sign that the body is fighting an infection. Most fevers in children are caused by common viral illnesses and settle within a few days.

## Looking after a child with a fever

Offer plenty of fluids and let your child rest. Dress them in light clothing and keep the room at a comfortable temperature. Do not use cold baths or sponging. Check on your child regularly, including during the night.

## Fever medicine

Ask a pharmacist or your child's doctor which fever medicine is suitable for your child's age and weight, and follow the instructions on the label exactly. Never give more than the stated dose.

## Signs of dehydration

Signs of dehydration include a dry mouth, no tears when crying, fewer wet nappies, sunken eyes and unusual drowsiness.

## When to seek medical care

Seek urgent medical advice for a baby under three months with any fever, a child whose fever lasts more than five days, a child who is not drinking, or a child who seems to be getting worse.

Call your local emergency service immediately if your child has a rash that does not fade when pressed, has difficulty breathing, has a seizure, is very difficult to wake, or has a stiff neck.

## Returning to school

Children can usually return to school or nursery once the fever has settled and they feel well enough.
