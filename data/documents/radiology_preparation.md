# Radiology Preparation Instructions

*Synthetic demo document for MedFlow General Hospital. Educational content only.*

## Preparing for an MRI scan

An MRI scan uses a strong magnet and radio waves to create detailed images. To prepare for an MRI, remove all metal objects, including jewellery, watches, hairpins, hearing aids and removable dental work. Wear comfortable clothing without metal zips or fasteners, or change into the gown provided.

Tell the radiology team before your scan if you have a pacemaker, cochlear implant, metal clips, joint replacement, or any metal fragments in your body, or if you might be pregnant. Most MRI scans do not require fasting, but your appointment letter will say if you need to stop eating beforehand.

The scanner is noisy and you will be given ear protection. You need to lie still for 20 to 60 minutes. Tell the team in advance if you feel anxious in enclosed spaces.

## Preparing for a CT scan

A CT scan uses X-rays to build cross-sectional images. You may be asked not to eat for a few hours before the scan, especially if contrast dye will be used. Tell the team about any kidney problems, diabetes medication, previous reaction to contrast dye, or possible pregnancy.

## Contrast dye

Some scans use a contrast dye given by injection or as a drink to make images clearer. You may feel a brief warm sensation. Drink plenty of water afterwards unless you have been told to limit fluids.

## X-ray

No special preparation is needed for most X-rays. You may be asked to remove jewellery and to wear a gown. Tell the radiographer if you are or might be pregnant.

## Ultrasound

Preparation depends on the area being examined. For an abdominal ultrasound you are usually asked to fast for six hours. For a pelvic ultrasound you are usually asked to arrive with a full bladder.

## On the day

Arrive 15 minutes before your appointment and bring your referral and photo ID. Radiology is located on Level B1 of the Main Building.

## Results

A radiologist reviews your images and sends a report to the doctor who referred you. Your doctor will discuss the results with you.

## When to seek medical care

After contrast dye, seek urgent care if you develop a rash, swelling, or difficulty breathing.
