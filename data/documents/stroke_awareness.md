# Stroke Awareness Guide

*Synthetic demo document for MedFlow General Hospital. Educational content only.*

## What is a stroke

A stroke happens when the blood supply to part of the brain is cut off, either by a blocked blood vessel or by bleeding. Brain cells begin to be damaged within minutes, so a stroke is always a medical emergency.

## Recognising a stroke

The main warning signs of a stroke can be remembered with the word FAST. Face: the face may droop on one side. Arms: the person may not be able to lift both arms and keep them raised. Speech: speech may be slurred or confused. Time: it is time to call your local emergency service immediately if you see any of these signs.

Other signs include sudden weakness or numbness on one side of the body, sudden loss of vision, sudden severe headache, dizziness and difficulty understanding others.

## Transient ischaemic attack

A transient ischaemic attack, sometimes called a mini-stroke, causes the same symptoms as a stroke but they pass within minutes or hours. It is a warning that a full stroke may follow and still needs urgent assessment.

## Reducing your risk

High blood pressure is the biggest risk factor for stroke. Managing blood pressure, cholesterol and diabetes, not smoking, staying active and limiting alcohol all reduce the risk.

## After a stroke

Recovery varies widely. Rehabilitation may include physiotherapy, speech and language therapy and occupational therapy, and usually begins in hospital.

## When to seek medical care

Call your local emergency service immediately if you suspect a stroke, even if the symptoms disappear. Do not wait and do not drive yourself.
