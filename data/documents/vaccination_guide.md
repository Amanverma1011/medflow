# Vaccination Guide

*Synthetic demo document for MedFlow General Hospital. Educational content only.*

## How vaccines work

A vaccine trains the immune system to recognise and fight a specific infection without causing the disease. If you later meet the real infection, your body can respond faster and more strongly.

## Routine vaccines

Children receive routine vaccines according to the national schedule. Adults may be offered boosters and seasonal vaccines such as the flu vaccine. Additional vaccines may be recommended in pregnancy, for older adults, for people with long-term conditions, and before travel.

## Common side effects

Common side effects are mild and short-lived. They include a sore arm, tiredness, mild fever and aching muscles for a day or two. These are signs that the immune system is responding.

## Who should seek advice first

Tell the vaccinator if you have had a serious allergic reaction to a previous vaccine, have a weakened immune system, are pregnant, or are unwell with a high fever on the day.

## Keeping a record

Keep your vaccination record safe and bring it to appointments. The Pediatrics department can provide a copy of your child's vaccination history.

## When to seek medical care

Seek emergency care immediately for difficulty breathing, swelling of the face or throat, or a widespread rash after a vaccine. Serious reactions are rare and usually happen within minutes, which is why you are asked to wait briefly after vaccination.
